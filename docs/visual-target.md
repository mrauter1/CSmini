Observations carried into the target summary:

- The set consistently lands on dusty concrete compounds, water towers, shutters, catwalks, and stacked crates.
- The UI direction is thin-bordered, dark, compact, and game-like rather than dashboard-like.
- The strongest images keep the weapon and character silhouettes faceted and readable, not glossy.
- Team-color accents work best as faded blue or muted warm accents rather than bright esports colors.

## Visual Target Summary

### Color Palette

- Base palette: beige, tan, dust brown, concrete gray, charcoal
- Supporting accents: olive, rust, faded blue, dark green, worn yellow
- Saturation rule: muted and sun-faded, with only brief bright accents for muzzle flash, hit cues, and small UI highlights

### Map Atmosphere

- The accepted atmosphere is a sun-baked industrial/desert compound with dusty air, worn surfaces, and old-PC roughness.
- The map should feel compact, tactical, and believable rather than cinematic or empty.
- Landmark types to preserve: water towers, catwalks, shutters, stacked crates, low barriers, painted directional markings, and tight service lanes.

### Geometry Style

- Geometry should stay low-poly, angular, squared-off, and readable from gameplay distance.
- Buildings should be simple concrete or plaster masses with restrained trim and obvious cover pieces.
- Cover pieces should be blocky and purposeful: crate stacks, low walls, ramps, door frames, and partial barriers.

### HUD/Menu Style

- Menus and HUD should use dark translucent or matte panels, thin borders, condensed text, and restrained color accents.
- Layout should feel functional and tactical: map list, preview panel, minimap, player list, health, ammo, and respawn state all read quickly.
- Text treatment should feel like an old PC mod or late-1990s/early-2000s game interface, not a modern product UI.

### Character Style

- Opposing players should read as boxy humanoid tactical figures with clear head, torso, arms, legs, and facing direction.
- Detail level should stay light: planar armor, cloth blocks, minimal pouches, team-color accents, and strong silhouette contrast against the environment.
- Faces should be simple or partially covered; no realistic modern-operator fetish detail.

### Weapon Style

- The first-person weapon should be an original abstract low-poly carbine/SMG built from simple faceted shapes.
- It should sit low-right, stay attached to the camera, and leave most of the screen readable.
- Feedback should be immediate but restrained: small recoil, compact muzzle flash, and subtle hit/death cues.

### Lighting Style

- Use practical daylight or warm industrial interior lighting with readable shadows and gentle haze.
- Keep contrast high enough to separate walkable space, cover, and characters, but avoid heavy bloom or cinematic post.
- Interiors can be slightly dimmer and warmer than exteriors, while still staying clean enough for combat readability.

### What Must Be Avoided

- Any Counter-Strike names, logos, copied map layouts, copied props, copied UI treatments, or direct asset lookalikes
- Photorealistic materials, glossy PBR-heavy rendering, dramatic post-processing, or modern military presentation
- Sci-fi architecture, fantasy props, voxel/Minecraft cues, cartoon/mobile shooter styling, or SaaS-like menu composition
- Random gray-box spaces with no landmark identity, route readability, or tactical cover logic
- Weapons or characters so detailed that they stop reading as lightweight browser-native low-poly assets
